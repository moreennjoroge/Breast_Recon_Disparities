# Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery (PRS) Manuscript Structure

## Standard Manuscript Components (in order)

### 1. Title Page
- Title: Clear, descriptive, includes study design (case-control, RCT, systematic review, etc.)
- Author names, qualifications, and affiliations
- Corresponding author contact information
- Word count of body and abstract
- Conflict of interest disclosures
- Authorship contribution statement (using CRediT guidelines)
- Funding/grant information

### 2. Structured Abstract (250 words maximum)
Required headings in order:
- **Background**: Problem being addressed, context/significance
- **Methods**: Study design, participants, interventions, analysis methods
- **Results**: Key findings with specific data/statistics
- **Conclusions**: Primary conclusions and clinical implications

### 3. Manuscript Body

#### Introduction Section
- Establish clinical problem or knowledge gap
- Brief literature review demonstrating gap
- State study objectives/hypothesis clearly
- Brief description of approach
- Length: typically 2-3 paragraphs, focused and concise

#### Methods Section
- Study design and setting
- Patient selection criteria (inclusion/exclusion)
- Ethical approval statement (IRB or equivalent)
- Surgical techniques or interventions (with sufficient detail for replication)
- Outcome measures (primary and secondary)
- Statistical analysis methods (specify tests used, significance level, software)
- Sample size calculation (if applicable)
- Follow-up period

**Statistical reporting requirements:**
- Name the statistical test used before presenting results
- State p-values with appropriate precision (e.g., p<0.001, p=0.04)
- Report confidence intervals where appropriate
- Identify the statistical software package used

#### Results Section
- Present findings in logical sequence
- Start with patient demographics and baseline characteristics
- Present primary outcome first, then secondary outcomes
- Use tables and figures to display data efficiently
- Report actual numbers, not just percentages
- Include p-values and confidence intervals
- Avoid interpretation—save for Discussion
- Typical length: 2-4 pages

#### Discussion Section
- Restate principal findings briefly
- Compare with existing literature
- Discuss clinical implications
- Address study limitations honestly
- Suggest future research directions
- Avoid repetition of results
- End with strong concluding statement
- Typical length: 3-5 pages

### 4. References
- Follow AMA citation style (superscript numbers in text)
- Cite in numerical order as they appear in text
- List all authors up to 6; if more than 6, list first 3 followed by "et al."
- Abbreviate journal names per Index Medicus
- Include DOIs when available
- Limit to most relevant, recent citations (typically 20-40 for original research)

#### Reference Format Examples:
**Journal article:**
Craft AB. Title with initial cap in Roman: Subtitle also with initial cap in Roman. Plast Reconstr Surg. 1997;100:111-115.

**Book chapter:**
Reagan MF. Title with initial cap: Subtitle with initial cap. In: Smith SR, Jones HG, Green TM, eds. Cap and Lower Case Title in Italics. Vol. 5, 5th ed. New York: Bender & Sons; 1971:22-50.

**Website:**
American Society of Plastic Surgeons. 2005 liposuction statistics. Available at: http://asps.org/stats.htm. Accessed March 4, 2015.

### 5. Tables
- Each table submitted as separate editable file (Word, WordPerfect, or RTF)
- Numbered in order of appearance
- Brief, descriptive title above table
- Footnotes below table for abbreviations or additional information
- Should stand alone without referring to text
- Use to present data efficiently, not duplicate text

### 6. Figure Legends
- Numbered to correspond with figures
- Brief descriptive text for each figure
- Include any necessary abbreviation definitions
- Note any permissions required for reused images
- Placed at end of manuscript document after references

### 7. Figures
- Submit each figure as separate file
- Acceptable formats: TIFF, JPEG, EPS, PDF
- Minimum resolution: 300 dpi
- Minimum width: 3.25 inches (5 inches for graphs/text)
- Color mode: CMYK
- Before/after photos must match in size, position, lighting
- No eye blocks or blurring—proper consent required
- Professional quality with clean backgrounds

## Article-Specific Requirements

### Original Research Articles
- Abstract: Structured (Background, Methods, Results, Conclusions)
- Word count: Typically 3000-5000 words
- References: 30-50 references
- Figures: 6-8 figure pieces
- Tables: 3-5 tables

### Review Articles
- Abstract: May be structured or unstructured
- Word count: 4000-6000 words
- References: 75-150 references (comprehensive)
- Figures: 4-6 figure pieces
- Tables: 2-4 tables

### Case Reports
- Abstract: Brief unstructured summary
- Word count: 1500-2500 words
- References: 10-20 references
- Figures: 3-6 figure pieces showing key clinical findings

## Level of Evidence (Through December 2025)

All clinical studies will be assigned a Level of Evidence (LOE) by an independent panel:

### Therapeutic Studies
- **Level I**: High-quality RCT with adequate power; or systematic review
- **Level II**: Lesser-quality RCT; prospective cohort; or systematic review
- **Level III**: Retrospective cohort or case-control study
- **Level IV**: Case series
- **Level V**: Expert opinion; case report; bench research

### Diagnostic Studies
- **Level I**: High-quality cohort validating diagnostic test
- **Level II**: Exploratory cohort developing diagnostic criteria
- **Level III**: Diagnostic study in non-consecutive patients
- **Level IV**: Case-control study
- **Level V**: Expert opinion; case report

### Prognostic/Risk Studies
- **Level I**: High-quality prospective cohort with adequate power
- **Level II**: Lesser-quality prospective cohort; retrospective cohort
- **Level III**: Case-control study
- **Level IV**: Case series
- **Level V**: Expert opinion; case report

## Ethical Requirements

### Human Subjects Research
- IRB or ethics committee approval required
- Include statement in Methods section
- Conform to Declaration of Helsinki
- Obtain informed consent from all participants

### Patient Photographs
- Written consent required for identifiable photos (face, visible tattoos, identifiable body parts)
- Use standardized PRS patient authorization form
- No eye blocks or blurring permitted
- Physical signatures or verified e-signatures required (no typed signatures)
- For deceased/incompetent patients: power of attorney consent required

### Animal Research
- Full compliance with ethical principles required
- Local licensing arrangements must be followed
- High standards of care and humanity required

## Common Manuscript Errors to Avoid

1. **Abstract errors:**
   - Exceeding 250 word limit
   - Missing required headings
   - Including citations or references
   - Presenting data not in main text

2. **Methods errors:**
   - Insufficient detail to replicate study
   - Missing ethical approval statement
   - Statistical methods not specified
   - No statement of significance level (α)

3. **Results errors:**
   - Interpreting results (belongs in Discussion)
   - Presenting percentages without absolute numbers
   - Missing p-values or confidence intervals
   - Figures/tables not cited in numerical order

4. **Figure errors:**
   - Resolution below 300 dpi
   - File format not acceptable
   - Before/after photos inconsistent
   - Missing patient consent
   - Unprofessional quality (messy fields, cluttered backgrounds)

5. **Reference errors:**
   - Not following AMA style
   - Citing references out of numerical order
   - Including too many historical references
   - Missing DOIs when available
