# Plastic Surgery Research Manuscript Writer

Transform research data into publication-ready manuscripts that meet the rigorous standards of leading plastic surgery journals.

## When to Use This Skill

Use this skill when:
- Writing original research manuscripts for plastic surgery journals
- Editing or improving existing research drafts
- Structuring study findings into proper manuscript format
- Ensuring compliance with journal-specific requirements
- Reviewing manuscripts before submission
- Transforming data/results into compelling narratives

## Core Philosophy

Effective plastic surgery research writing balances:
1. **Scientific rigor** - Precise methodology, appropriate statistics, honest limitations
2. **Clinical relevance** - Clear implications for practice, patient care considerations
3. **Narrative clarity** - Logical flow, concise language, accessible to specialists
4. **Professional standards** - Journal formatting, ethical compliance, complete reporting

## Manuscript Development Workflow

### Step 1: Understand the Research
Before writing, clarify:
- Study design and objectives
- Key findings and their significance
- Target journal (determines structure and word limits)
- Intended audience within plastic surgery

### Step 2: Select Appropriate Reference Materials
Based on the writing task, read relevant reference files:
- **Always read first:** `/references/prs-manuscript-structure.md` for standard manuscript organization
- **For writing style guidance:** `/references/scientific-writing-style.md` for section-specific writing patterns
- **For study design reporting:** `/references/reporting-guidelines.md` for appropriate checklists (STROBE, CONSORT, CARE, etc.)

### Step 3: Structure the Manuscript
Follow the standard IMRAD format:
1. Title and Abstract
2. Introduction
3. Methods
4. Results  
5. Discussion
6. References
7. Tables and Figures

### Step 4: Write Section by Section
Develop each section following best practices from the reference materials, maintaining:
- Appropriate verb tense for each section
- Proper citation style (AMA format)
- Logical flow and transitions
- Professional, objective tone

### Step 5: Review and Refine
Check against journal requirements:
- Word count limits
- Abstract structure and length
- Figure/table limits
- Reference formatting
- Ethical statements
- Supplemental material

## Key Principles for Each Section

### Title
- Include study design (retrospective cohort, RCT, systematic review)
- Be specific about intervention and population
- Keep concise (12-20 words ideal)
- Optimize for searchability with key terms

### Abstract (250 words maximum)
**Required structure for original research:**
- **Background:** Problem and study objective (2-3 sentences)
- **Methods:** Design, participants, intervention, analysis (3-4 sentences)
- **Results:** Key findings with specific data (3-4 sentences)
- **Conclusions:** Primary implications (2-3 sentences)

**Writing approach:**
- Make it standalone—readers may only see abstract
- Include specific numbers and statistics
- No citations or references
- Present tense for conclusions, past tense for what was done

### Introduction (2-3 paragraphs)
**Structure:**
1. Clinical significance of problem
2. Current state of knowledge (brief literature review)
3. Knowledge gap being addressed
4. Study objective/hypothesis

**Writing approach:**
- Start broad, narrow to specific question
- Cite only most relevant, recent papers
- Make the "gap" explicit
- End with clear objective statement

### Methods
**Essential components:**
- Study design (stated upfront)
- Setting and timeframe
- Patient selection (inclusion/exclusion criteria)
- Ethical approval statement (IRB/ethics committee)
- Surgical techniques or interventions (sufficient detail for replication)
- Outcome measures (primary and secondary, defined operationally)
- Statistical analysis (tests used, significance level, software)

**Writing approach:**
- Use past tense throughout
- Be specific with numbers and definitions
- State statistical methods before presenting results needing them
- Include enough detail for replication
- Define all abbreviations on first use

### Results (2-4 pages typically)
**Standard sequence:**
1. Patient demographics and baseline characteristics (Table 1)
2. Primary outcome
3. Secondary outcomes
4. Complications or adverse events
5. Subgroup analyses (if applicable)

**Writing approach:**
- Present objectively without interpretation
- Cite tables/figures to support text
- Report actual numbers with percentages
- Include p-values and confidence intervals
- Past tense for what was found, present tense for data in tables
- No hedging—state findings directly

### Discussion (3-5 pages typically)
**Standard structure:**
1. Principal findings restated briefly (1 paragraph)
2. Comparison with existing literature (2-3 paragraphs)
3. Explanation of findings (1-2 paragraphs)
4. Clinical implications (1-2 paragraphs)
5. Limitations (1 paragraph)
6. Future directions (optional, 1 paragraph)
7. Conclusions (1 paragraph)

**Writing approach:**
- Do not repeat detailed results
- Compare findings to similar studies
- Explain mechanisms when appropriate
- Be honest and specific about limitations
- Avoid overstating conclusions
- Balance optimism with objectivity
- Present tense for general statements, past for study-specific

### References
- Follow AMA citation style (superscript numbers)
- Cite in numerical order in text
- List up to 6 authors; >6 use first 3 plus "et al."
- Abbreviate journals per Index Medicus
- Include DOIs when available
- Limit to most relevant citations (typically 20-50 for original research)

## Common Excellence Indicators

**Highly publishable manuscripts demonstrate:**
- Clear clinical relevance and novelty
- Rigorous methodology with appropriate sample size
- Complete statistical reporting with effect sizes and CIs
- Honest acknowledgment of limitations
- Professional figures meeting quality standards (300 dpi, CMYK)
- Proper ethical compliance documented
- Adherence to reporting guidelines (STROBE, CONSORT, etc.)
- Concise, clear writing without redundancy
- Logical flow and organization

## Frequent Pitfalls to Avoid

**Abstract errors:**
- Exceeding 250 words
- Missing required structural headings
- Vague conclusions without data

**Methods errors:**
- Insufficient procedural detail
- Statistical methods not specified
- Missing ethical approval statement
- Undefined outcome measures

**Results errors:**
- Interpreting findings (belongs in Discussion)
- Percentages without absolute numbers
- Missing measures of precision (CI, p-values)
- Figures/tables cited out of order

**Discussion errors:**
- Repeating results in detail
- Ignoring contradictory literature
- Overstating conclusions
- Vague limitations
- Missing clinical implications

**Figure errors:**
- Resolution below 300 dpi
- Inconsistent before/after photography
- Missing patient consent
- Unprofessional quality

## Journal-Specific Considerations

### PRS (Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery)
- Highest tier—expects originality and high impact
- Rigorous peer review including biostatistician
- Level of Evidence assigned through December 2025
- Structured abstract required for original articles
- Emphasis on multicenter studies and large cohorts
- CME/MOC articles available

### JPRAS (Journal of Plastic, Reconstructive & Aesthetic Surgery)  
- Strong international reputation (BAPRAS official journal)
- Accepts case reports, technical innovations, and original research
- Graphical abstracts encouraged
- References to previous JPRAS articles expected
- High ethical standards for human/animal studies

### Annals of Plastic Surgery
- Practical focus on clinical applications
- Case series and technical innovations welcome
- Abstract 200-250 words
- Vancouver referencing system
- Open access with APC

### Journal of Craniofacial Surgery
- Specialist focus on craniofacial and pediatric plastic surgery
- Long-term follow-up valued
- Multidisciplinary perspectives encouraged
- Growth and development considerations important

## Tone and Voice

Write with:
- **Confidence without arrogance** - "This study demonstrates" not "We believe we have shown"
- **Objectivity** - Avoid emotive language ("surprisingly," "unfortunately")
- **Precision** - Specific numbers rather than vague quantifiers
- **Clarity** - Accessible to plastic surgery specialists without oversimplification
- **Professionalism** - Respectful of previous work, balanced in interpretation

## Quality Assurance Checklist

Before finalizing:
- [ ] Study design stated clearly in title and methods
- [ ] Abstract follows required structure (250 words max)
- [ ] Methods enable replication
- [ ] Statistical analysis appropriate and fully described
- [ ] Results presented objectively with complete data
- [ ] Discussion interprets without repeating
- [ ] Limitations acknowledged specifically
- [ ] All figures/tables cited in numerical order
- [ ] References follow AMA format
- [ ] Word count within limits
- [ ] Ethical approvals documented
- [ ] Patient consents obtained
- [ ] Appropriate reporting guideline checklist completed
