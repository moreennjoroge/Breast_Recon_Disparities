## ABSTRACT

**Background:** Despite the known psychosocial benefits of post-mastectomy breast reconstruction, Black women undergo this procedure at significantly lower rates than White women. This qualitative study investigates the personal, interpersonal, and systemic factors influencing breast reconstruction decision-making among Black women.

**Methods:** We conducted semi-structured interviews with 11 Black women (median age: 45 years, IQR: 10) who were considering or had undergone breast reconstruction following a breast cancer diagnosis. The interviews were transcribed and analyzed using thematic content analysis.

**Results:** Three key themes emerged from the analysis: (1) patient-provider communication, where culturally sensitive interactions and race-concordant visual aids were identified as crucial for informed decision-making; (2) financial barriers, with participants expressing concerns about lost income, childcare, and other out-of-pocket expenses; and (3) medical mistrust, which was rooted in historical racial disparities in healthcare. Furthermore, participants reported feeling unprepared for the physical and emotional toll of breast reconstruction, underscoring the need for more comprehensive preoperative counseling.

**Conclusion:** Black women face unique challenges in the breast reconstruction decision-making process, driven by communication barriers, financial strain, and medical mistrust. Addressing these challenges through culturally sensitive communication, comprehensive financial counseling, and efforts to reduce medical mistrust may help mitigate disparities in breast reconstruction uptake and improve the overall quality of care.

**Keywords:** breast reconstruction, breast cancer, racial disparities, health equity, qualitative research, patient-provider communication
